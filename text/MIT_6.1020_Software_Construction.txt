Programming is a craft.
It comes down to getting a computer to do what you/user wants it to do.

As a programmer, you are part listener, part advisor, part interpreter, and part dictator.

You try to capture elusive requirement and find a way of expressing them so that a mere machine can do them justice.
You try to document you work so that others can understand it, and you try to engineer you work so that others can build on it.
You try to do all this again the relentless ticking of the project clock.
You work small miracles every day.

It's a difficult job.

There are many people offering you help.
Tool vendors tout the miracles their products perform.
Methodology gurus promise that their techniques guarantee results.
Everyone claims that their programming language is the best, and every OS is the answer to all conceivable ills.

There are no easy answers. There is no such thing as a best solution, be it a tool, a language, or an OS.
There can only be systems that are more appropriate in a particular set of circumstances.

Who should read this book?
- You fell frustrated that you don't seem to be achieving your potential.
- You look at colleagues who seem to be using tools to make themselves more productive than you.
- You current job uses older technologies.

What makes a pragmatic programmer?
- Each developer is unique, with individual strengths and weaknesses
- Over time, each will craft his/her own personal environment. It will reflect the programmer's individuality just 
  as forcefully as his/her hobbies, clothing, or haircut.
- Early adapter / fast adapter.
- Inquisitive. Ask questions.
- Critical thinker.
- Realistic.
- Jack of all trades.

Tips:
- Care about your craft.
- Think! About your work. To think about what you're doing while you're doing it.

The construction of software should be an engineering discipline.
Within the overall structure of a project there is always room for individuality and craftsmanship.

Chapter 1.
A pragmatic phylosophy
----------------------

What distinguishes pragmatic programmers?
- It's an attitude
- It's a style
- It's a philosophy of approaching problems and their solutions.

They think beyond the immediate problem, always trying to place it in its larger context, always trying to be aware of the bigger picture.

They take responsibility for everything they do.

Learning is a continuous and ongoing process.

Communicate!

Entropy is amount of disorder in a system.
When disorder increases in software, programmers call it software rot:
 - psychology;
 - culture;
 - work on a project.
 - broken window theory

Psychology of Debugging:
Debugging itself is a sensitive, emotional subject for many developers.
Instead of attacking it as a puzzle to be solved, you may encounter denial, finger pointing, lame excuses, or
just plain apathy.

Embrace the fact that debugging is just problem solving, and attack it as such.

Tip 24: Fix the problem, not the blame

Above all, remember the first rule of debugging: don't panic.
